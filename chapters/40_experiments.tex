\section{Experiments}
In order to assess the efficacy of the codec, a series of benchmarks will be presented, 
with a view to drawing comparisons with a number of popular image compression schemes. 
It is acknowledged that FRIF is capable of operating in both lossless and lossy modes and 
experiments are conducted separately for each mode. Performance was measured across
kodak [citation] and CID22 [citation] datasets, which are designed to contain a variety 
of different image types.

\subsection{Lossless compression}

Lossless compression mode performance is measured solely with average amount bits required to decode one pixel
of 24 bit depth for RGB8 pictures. Measurement of FRIF was done along PNG, JPEG2000 and QOI codecs to draw the comparisons.
\begin{figure}[ht]
    \begin{center}
        \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/experimental/bench_lossless_kodak.png}
    \end{center}
    \caption{Bits per pixel benchmark across PNG, JPEG2000 and proposed FRIF on kodak dataset. Noticably on
    almost all samples FRIF achieves lower bpp values than PNG. On all samples JPEG2000 achieves best 
    compression ratios}\label{fig:results_kodak}
\end{figure}

As shown on Figure~\ref{fig:results_kodak} with its current form and implementation lossless FRIF exceedes PNG in compression
rates on highly detailed images available in kodak dataset. However this advantage diminishes
on images with higher amounts of smooth areas like in CID22 or J31 dataset as shown in Table~\ref{tab:results_lossless}. 
\begin{table}[ht]
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tabular}[c]{|l||l|l|l|}
            \hline
            \textbf{codec} & \textbf{kodak} & \textbf{CID22} & \textbf{J31}\\
            \hline
            png & $13.049914$ & $10.99834$ & $12.812785$\\
            frif & $11.59786$ & $11.559122$ & $13.246048$\\
            jpeg2000 & $9.57616$ & $8.873637$ & $11.15974$\\            
            qoi & $13.988512$ & $13.283921$ & $16.568345$\\
            \hline
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    \caption{Average bits per pixel across all images in datasets}\label{tab:results_lossless}
\end{table}
