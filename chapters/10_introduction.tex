\section{Introduction}

When we started storing images digitally we noticed that they take way too much space given how 
little information they contain. In natural non-random images there is a tendency of similarity between
surrounding pixels, if we take one pixel from the area that represents the sky we have great chance
to see very similiar colour in the neighbourhood. Image compression algorithms strives from those redundancies
by transforming input image into an intermediate representation, that has better statistical parameters for
entropy encoding. Historically most popular such transformations were DCT (Discrete Cosine Transform) and DWT
(Discrete Wavelet Transform), both of which transform the input space by dividing it into a grid of blocks and then
performing futher operations within each one. Such transformed image still contains a lot of information that is missed
by human vision. High frequency elements are less seen by visual cortex and to futher compress an image 
the transformed representation is quantizied in lossy codecs by eliminating those unseen values. 
This approach proved to achieve best results in modern codecs, but with harsher
quantization in lossy compression block boundaries are much more visible, damaging the quality. 
Lossless codecs often used block lattices for context modelling and better prediction of residuals. Each block is directly adjacent 
to 4 different blocks and can infer its values from those neighbours. In JPEG-LS [citation] authors proposed a predictor
LOCO-I that assigns each element to one of 365 contexts based on the already decoded neighbouring values adjusting 
the distributions of encoded elements. Such grouping enables codec to use different instances of entropy coder for 
different propability distributions observed within each context.

A different approach can be seen in JPEGXL: they use the DCT over blocks of variadic size. Larger monotonic
regions can be covered by a larger blocks, aggregating similar propability distributions together.

Building ahead of this ideas and problems I will present a novel image compression codec \textbf{FRIF} (\textbf{FR}actal \textbf{I}mage \textbf{F}ormat)
which presents a new idea of lattice decomposition of an image with discrete wavelet transform applied with each fractal. Such grid
has a certain amount of interesting properties that could be leveraged in mentioned image compression methods:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Complex numeral systems fractals have a highly irregular boundary
    \item Since each fractal has 6 immiediate neighbours, we get an hexagonal grid which allow to infer contexts and 
        predictions from larger set
    \item Self-similarity relation of each fractal can benefit from wavelet transform decomposition
\end{itemize}

Along with whitepaper I will present an implementation of the codec with comparisons to the existing lossless and lossy
popular approaches.
I will conduct experiments over 2 datasets: JSI31[citation] and JPEG dataset[citation] and collect
PSNR and SSIM2 metrics that corresponents to image quality after compression.

Aim of this thesis is not to beat current sophisticated compression models with FRIF, but 
to measure performance of a quite different approach. The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows.
First section presents the preliminaries required to state the compression algorithm, including complex-based numeral systems, wavelet transform and chosen entropy encoder.
Section 2 fully presents FRIF compression algorithm, including all stages of compression and chosen image format to store compressed images on disc, giving the official
and full specification of this compression scheme. 
Section 3 showcases experiments taken to measure performance of codec, including metrics like bits per pixel or the quality of compressed image on lossy compression. 
Finally section 4 reviews the codec and states the conclusion of this novel and different approach.
